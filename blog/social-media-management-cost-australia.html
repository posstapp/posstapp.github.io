How Much Does Social Media Management Cost in Australia?

posst.app · July 2026 · 7 min read

If you're a small business owner in Australia, you've probably thought about outsourcing your social media. Maybe you've Googled a few agencies, asked around, or got a quote that made you close the laptop and go back to doing it yourself.

You're not imagining it - social media management is expensive. But the cost depends entirely on what you're buying and who you're buying it from. This guide breaks down the real numbers in 2026 so you can make an informed decision.

The short answer

For a typical Australian small business posting to Facebook and Instagram, here's what you'll pay depending on the approach:

DIY with free tools: A$0 per month - plus 8 to 15 hours of your time every week. Meta Business Suite is free but you still have to write captions, create images, and remember to post.

Scheduling tools (Buffer, Later, Hootsuite): A$20 to A$200 per month for the software, but you're still creating all the content yourself. These tools handle the "when" but not the "what."

Freelance social media manager: A$500 to A$1,500 per month. You'll typically get 2 to 4 posts per week, template-based graphics, a content calendar, and a monthly summary. Strategy and original photography are usually extra.

Agency management: A$1,200 to A$3,000+ per month. This is where you start getting strategy sessions, professional content creation, community management, and detailed reporting. Most agencies in Australia charge between A$1,500 and A$2,500 per month for a single platform.

AI-powered automation (like posst.app): A$25 to A$35 per month. Fully automated daily posts to Facebook and Instagram - AI-generated captions and images, published without your involvement. No content creation, no scheduling, no approvals.

Why the gap is so large

There's a reason agency pricing starts at four figures. You're paying for a human being to think about your business, write in your voice, create visuals, schedule posts, respond to comments, and report on what's working. That takes real time, and time costs money.

A freelancer posting three times a week to two platforms is doing roughly 6 to 10 hours of work per month on your account. At even modest Australian freelance rates, that's A$600 to A$1,000 before any overheads.

Agencies add strategy, account management, creative direction, and reporting on top of that. Their pricing reflects the team behind the work.

The question isn't whether agencies and freelancers are overcharging - most aren't. The question is whether a small business with annual revenue under A$500,000 actually needs that level of service.

What most small businesses actually need

Here's the uncomfortable truth: the majority of small businesses don't need a social media strategist. They need to show up. Consistently. Every day or close to it.

A café in Fitzroy doesn't need a A$2,000-per-month agency running competitive analysis and building audience personas. It needs a post going out every morning showing today's special, a seasonal coffee tip, or a shot of the shop floor - something that tells people on Instagram "yes, we're open, and we're worth visiting."

A dog groomer in Hawthorn doesn't need a content calendar with quarterly strategy reviews. They need daily posts about coat care, breed-specific grooming tips, and local dog park recommendations - content that shows expertise and keeps the business visible in local feeds.

For businesses like these, the real cost of not posting is bigger than the cost of any tool. A dormant social profile tells potential customers you might not be operating anymore. And every day you don't post, your competitor who does moves one spot ahead of you in the algorithm.

The hidden cost: your own time

The most expensive option is often the one that looks free - doing it yourself.

When a business owner spends 10 hours a week on social media, that's 10 hours not spent on billable work, client calls, or growing the business. If your time is worth A$50 an hour (conservative for most business owners), you're spending A$2,000 a month in opportunity cost - more than most agencies charge.

And the reality is, most owners don't spend 10 hours. They spend one frantic hour on Sunday night, schedule three posts, feel guilty by Wednesday, and go quiet by Thursday. The "free" option usually means irregular posting, which is worse than not being on social media at all.

Comparing your real options

Here's how the main approaches stack up for a small business posting daily to Facebook and Instagram:

Do it yourself: A$0 out of pocket, but 8 to 15 hours of your time weekly. Posting quality varies. Consistency is the biggest challenge - most businesses stop within a few weeks.

Scheduling tool: A$20 to A$200 per month. You still create everything. The tool just automates the "publish" step, not the "think, write, design" steps.

Freelancer: A$500 to A$1,500 per month. Better consistency, but typically 3 to 4 posts per week, not daily. Template-based visuals. You'll need to provide direction and feedback.

Agency: A$1,200 to A$3,000+ per month. Full strategy and execution. Best quality, but expensive for most small businesses and often includes more than you need.

AI automation: A$25 to A$35 per month. Daily posts, fully automated. AI-generated captions and images in your brand voice. Zero time required after setup. Content is good enough to maintain a consistent, active presence - which is what the algorithm and local customers care about most.

Which option makes sense for you?

If you're a larger business with A$10,000+ monthly marketing budgets and need campaign strategy, influencer coordination, and paid ad management - hire an agency. That's what they're built for.

If you're a growing business that can afford A$1,000 to A$1,500 per month and wants a human touch on every post - a good freelancer is solid value.

If you're a local business - a tradie, a café, a groomer, a physio, a retail shop - and you just need to be visible on social media every day without it eating into your workday, AI automation at A$25 to A$35 per month is the most practical choice.
