Social Media for Cafes and Restaurants: A Practical Guide

If you run a cafe or restaurant in Australia, you already know how competitive the hospitality game is. A great espresso or a killer Sunday brunch menu is no longer enough on its own. Your potential customers are scrolling Instagram before they decide where to eat, checking Google reviews, and watching TikToks of someone else's perfectly plated avocado toast. If you are not showing up in those moments, someone else is.

This guide cuts through the noise and gives you a realistic, practical look at social media for cafes and restaurants - what actually works, what is a waste of time, and how to make it sustainable when you are already run off your feet.

Why Social Media Actually Matters for Hospitality

Let us be honest. Most cafe and restaurant owners did not start their business because they wanted to become content creators. But here is the reality: around 74% of diners check a restaurant's social media before visiting for the first time. In dense, competitive food scenes like Melbourne's Fitzroy, Sydney's Surry Hills, or Brisbane's Fortitude Valley, a dormant Instagram account can genuinely cost you customers.

Social media for cafes and restaurants is not just about vanity metrics. It drives foot traffic, builds loyalty, and gives you a direct line to your community. When you post that your house-made tiramisu just came back on the menu, your regulars see it and come in that week. That is real, measurable value.

The flip side? Doing it badly - blurry photos, inconsistent posting, ghosting your comments - can actually hurt your reputation. So let us talk about how to do it properly.

What Platforms Should You Actually Be On

You do not need to be everywhere. You need to be where your customers are.

Instagram

Still the king for food businesses. High-quality food photography, Stories for behind-the-scenes content, and Reels for short videos that can reach new audiences. If you are only going to commit to one platform, make it this one.

Facebook

Do not write it off. Plenty of Australian local community groups, suburb-based food pages, and older demographics still live on Facebook. It is also useful for events, promotions, and connecting with local families. A cafe in a suburb like Hawthorn or Newtown with a strong local community will often find Facebook surprisingly effective.

TikTok

If you have a team member who is comfortable on camera and you can invest time into short video content, TikTok can generate extraordinary reach for almost nothing. A restaurant in Perth showing the process of making their wood-fired sourdough could easily hit tens of thousands of views. But it takes consistency and creativity, so only add this if you can genuinely commit.

Google Business Profile

Technically not social media, but do not overlook it. Keeping your photos updated and responding to reviews is essential for local search. It is often the first thing people see.

What Content Actually Works

This is where most small business owners get stuck. You know you should be posting, but what?

The content that performs best for hospitality businesses falls into a few reliable categories.

Food and drink photography is the obvious one - and it still works brilliantly when done well. You do not need a professional photographer every week. A modern smartphone, decent natural light (morning is usually best), and a clean background will get you 80% of the way there. Show the steam rising from a coffee, the cross-section of a burger, the colour of a vibrant smoothie bowl.

Behind-the-scenes content builds trust and personality. Show your chef prepping the specials at 7am. Introduce your barista. Film the weekly delivery of fresh produce from your local supplier. People connect with people, not just plates.

Specials and limited-time offers create urgency. Post your weekend special on a Thursday or Friday. Announce a new menu item the day it launches. Let your followers feel like they are getting insider access.

User-generated content is free marketing gold. When customers tag you in their posts, reshare it (with credit). It is authentic, it is social proof, and it costs you nothing.

Local community moments matter too. Sponsoring a local sports team? Hosting a charity morning tea? These posts show you are part of the neighbourhood, which builds the kind of loyalty that keeps people coming back.

The Real Challenge - Consistency

Here is the hard truth about social media for cafes and restaurants: posting once a fortnight is not a strategy. Algorithms reward consistency, and so do customers. But when you are managing staff rosters, dealing with suppliers, handling a busy Saturday service, and trying to have some kind of life outside work, finding time to create and schedule content is genuinely difficult.

This is why so many hospitality businesses fall into the trap of bursts of activity followed by weeks of silence. It is also why some look at hiring a social media agency - and find out pretty quickly that quality agencies in Australia typically charge between $800 and $2,000 or more per month. For a small independent cafe turning over $600,000 a year, that is a significant chunk of your margin.

The good news is that technology has made it much easier to manage your own social presence without spending hours on it every week.

How to Build a Simple Content System

You do not need a content strategy document the size of a novel. You need a simple repeatable system.

Start by batching your content creation. Pick one time per week - maybe a quiet Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning before the lunch rush - and create content for the whole week in one go. Take photos of three or four dishes. Film a short behind-the-scenes clip. Write your captions. Then schedule everything to go out automatically.

A content calendar does not have to be fancy. A simple note on your phone or a whiteboard in the office with Monday through Sunday mapped out is enough to start.

Think in themes to make it easier. Mondays could be a weekly special. Wednesdays could be a team highlight. Fridays could be a weekend event or offer. Themes take the decision-making out of it, which is half the battle.

Use scheduling tools so you are not manually posting every day. There are various platforms designed to help small businesses stay consistent without being glued to their phone - some built specifically around AI-assisted content creation so you spend even less time staring at a blank screen wondering what to write.

Getting Engagement Without Begging for It

Posting great content is one thing. Getting people to actually engage with it is another.

Ask questions in your captions. "Which would you choose - the pulled pork or the brisket?" is more likely to get comments than just describing the dish. Polls in Instagram Stories are brilliant for this - quick, low-effort for your audience, and genuinely useful market research for you.

Reply to every comment when you are starting out. It signals to the algorithm that your content is worth showing, and it makes people feel seen. Even a simple "Thanks so much, see you soon!" goes a long way.

Use local hashtags. In addition to food-specific hashtags, use location-based ones relevant to your suburb and city. Customers searching for cafes in Paddington or Fremantle are actively looking for you.

Tag local suppliers, producers, and neighbouring businesses where relevant. It often leads to reshares and introduces your account to new audiences who are already interested in local food culture.

The Bottom Line

Social media for cafes and restaurants is not optional anymore - it is just part of running a food business in 2024. The good news is that it does not have to be overwhelming, expensive, or time-consuming if you approach it with a simple system and the right tools.

Focus on one or two platforms. Post consistently rather than perfectly. Show your food, your people, and your personality. Engage with your local community. And do not try to do everything - a cafe in Collingwood with a warm, consistent Instagram presence will beat a competitor posting sporadically across five platforms every single time.

You have already built something worth visiting. Now make sure people can find it.
