Social Media for Dog Groomers: What to Post and Why It Matters

If you run a dog grooming business in Australia, chances are you already know that word of mouth is gold. But in 2024, word of mouth has moved online - and it lives on Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok. The good news? Social media for dog groomers is one of the easiest niches to make work, because let's be honest, people cannot stop sharing photos of their dogs.

This guide breaks down exactly what to post, how often, and why it actually matters for your bookings and bottom line.

---

Why Social Media Actually Moves the Needle for Groomers

Dog owners are not just looking for a groomer - they are looking for someone they trust with their pet. Social media lets you build that trust before a customer ever picks up the phone or books online.

Think about it from a customer's perspective. If someone in Chatswood is searching for a local groomer, they might find two businesses with similar prices. One has an Instagram full of happy dogs, before-and-after transformations, and a friendly vibe. The other has not posted since 2021. The choice is obvious.

Social proof is everything in this industry. A consistent, active social presence tells potential clients that you are professional, that you care about animals, and that real people trust you with their pets. That is hard to fake, and it is hard to replicate with just a website.

---

The Content That Performs Best for Dog Groomers

You do not need a content strategy degree to figure this out. The content that works for groomers is pretty straightforward.

Before and After Photos

This is the bread and butter of social media for dog groomers. A scruffy cavoodle walking in and a fluffy, clean, styled cavoodle walking out - that is content that people save, share, and comment on. Always get permission from pet owners before posting, and consider tagging them if they are happy to be tagged. That expands your reach instantly.

Short Video Clips

Reels and TikToks of the grooming process perform exceptionally well. You do not need fancy equipment - a phone mount and decent lighting will do. Show the wash, the blow-dry, the final trim. Add a trending audio track and you have got content that the algorithm will actually push to new audiences.

Meet the Team and Behind the Scenes

People want to see who is handling their dog. A quick video introducing yourself or a staff member, showing your setup, or explaining how you handle anxious dogs builds enormous trust. Groomers in suburbs like Fitzroy, Manly, or West End who show their personality online tend to develop strong local followings.

Client Dog Spotlights

Ask regular clients if you can feature their dog. "Meet Biscuit, our favourite Monday morning regular" - that kind of post gets shared by the owner to their entire network. It is free marketing and it feels genuine because it is.

Seasonal and Topical Content

Summer coat maintenance tips. What to do before the school holidays if you want a grooming appointment. Why regular grooming matters for double-coated breeds in Australian heat. This kind of educational content positions you as an expert, not just a service provider.

---

How Often Should You Actually Post?

Consistency beats frequency every time. Posting three times a week reliably will outperform posting seven times one week and then going quiet for a month.

For most grooming businesses, a realistic and effective schedule looks something like this:

- Instagram and Facebook: three to four times per week

- Stories: daily if possible, even just a quick clip or poll

- TikTok or Reels: one to two times per week if you have video content

The challenge for most small business owners is not knowing what to post - it is finding the time to actually do it consistently while also running a full appointment book. That is where scheduling tools become genuinely useful.

Common Mistakes Dog Groomers Make on Social Media

Even groomers who are actively posting often make a few avoidable mistakes.

Inconsistent Branding

Your Instagram grid does not need to look like a magazine editorial, but it should feel cohesive. Stick to similar filters, similar caption styles, and a consistent tone. If your grid looks chaotic, potential clients might assume your business is too.

Only Posting Promotions

If every post is "20% off this week only" or "book now, limited spots", people will tune out or unfollow. The rule of thumb is roughly 80% value and engaging content, 20% promotional. Give people a reason to follow you beyond discounts.

Ignoring Comments and Messages

Social media for dog groomers works best when it is actually social. If someone comments "OMG that is adorable" on your post and you never respond, you are missing a chance to build a relationship. If someone DMs asking about pricing and you respond two days later, you have probably lost a booking. Set aside ten minutes a day to engage with your audience.

Not Using Location Tags

Australian dog owners are searching locally. Tag your suburb, your city, and use local hashtags. A Labrador owner in Paddington is not going to drive to Penrith for a groom. Make it easy for people nearby to find you.

---

Building a Local Following That Actually Books Appointments

Growing followers is nice, but growing a local audience that converts to paying clients is the real goal.

A few things that help:

Collaborate with local pet businesses. The doggy day care down the road, the vet clinic nearby, the pet supply store in your strip - these are all natural partners. Cross-promote each other's content and share audiences.

Run local giveaways. "Win a free groom for your dog - follow us and tag a friend in Northcote who has a dog." This kind of campaign is low cost and builds genuine local reach.

Encourage reviews and check-ins. Ask happy clients to leave a Google review or check in on Facebook when they pick up their dog. User-generated content and reviews are among the most trusted forms of social proof in 2024.
