How to Get More Customers from Facebook as a Small Business

If you run a small business in Australia, chances are you already know Facebook is where your customers are spending time. But knowing that and actually turning it into real sales are two very different things. Most small business owners post a few times, get a handful of likes from family and friends, then wonder why nobody is walking through the door or landing on their website.

The good news is that learning how to get more customers from Facebook as a small business does not require a massive budget or a marketing degree. It just requires the right approach - and a bit of consistency.

Here is what actually works.

Set Up Your Facebook Page Like a Business, Not a Hobby

Before you worry about content or ads, your Facebook Page needs to look the part. Think of it as your digital shopfront. If someone lands on a half-finished page with no cover photo, an outdated phone number and three posts from 2022, they will click away.

Here is a quick checklist:

- Use a clear, high-quality profile photo (your logo works well)

- Write a short, specific description that tells people exactly what you do and where you are based

- Add your correct suburb, opening hours, website and phone number

- Choose the right page category (Restaurant, Retail, Service Business, etc.)

- Enable the relevant call-to-action button - "Book Now", "Call Now", "Send Message", or "Shop Now"

A bakery in Fitzroy and a plumber in Parramatta will both benefit from the same basics here. Customers want to quickly confirm you are real, you are local and you can help them. Make that easy.

Post Content That Actually Earns Attention

This is where most small businesses go wrong. They post about themselves constantly - "We are open!", "Check out our new product!" - and wonder why engagement is low.

Facebook rewards content that people want to interact with. That means posts that entertain, inform, or make people feel something. Here is a practical breakdown of what tends to work well:

Show the people behind the business

A photo of your team on a busy Saturday morning. A quick video of you making your product. A behind-the-scenes look at how you prepare for a big event. People buy from people they like and trust, and this kind of content builds that connection fast.

Solve a small problem

A garden centre in the Hills District could post "Three reasons your natives are turning yellow (and what to do about it)." A bookkeeper in Fremantle could share "The one thing most sole traders forget to track before tax time." Useful content positions you as the expert and gives people a reason to follow you.

Use local references

Mention your suburb, your local events, your community. "Getting ready for the Manly Food Festival this weekend - come find us at stall 14." Posts like this feel real and relevant, and they attract exactly the local audience you want.

The general rule of thumb is to follow an 80/20 split - 80% helpful or interesting content, 20% direct promotion. If every post is a sales pitch, people will tune out.

Use Facebook Groups to Build Local Presence

Facebook Groups are one of the most underused tools for small business owners. There are active local community groups for almost every suburb and town in Australia - Mosman Mums, Inner West Buy Swap Sell, Ballarat Small Business Network - the list goes on.

These groups are full of your potential customers having real conversations every day.

The key is to join as a genuine community member, not a spammer. Answer questions in your area of expertise. Offer helpful advice. If someone asks "Does anyone know a good electrician near Ascot Vale?" and you are that electrician, reply professionally and let your profile do the selling.

Many group admins allow occasional business posts or have dedicated "business promotion" days. Respect the rules, be consistent and over time you will become the go-to person in your category for that community.

Run Facebook Ads Without Wasting Money

Organic reach on Facebook has declined over the years, and the honest truth is that a small paid budget can dramatically amplify your results. You do not need to spend a fortune. Many small businesses in Australia see strong returns starting at $10 to $20 AUD per day, especially when their targeting is tight.

Here are the basics to get you started:

Boost posts that are already performing

Rather than creating ads from scratch, take a post that is already getting decent organic engagement and put $50 to $100 behind it. You already know the content resonates - now you are just extending its reach.

Target your actual audience

Facebook's ad targeting lets you narrow by location (right down to a specific postcode), age, interests and behaviours. A children's clothing boutique in Chadstone can target women aged 25 to 45 within a 10 kilometre radius. A tradie in Brisbane can target homeowners in specific suburbs who have recently searched for renovation content.

Use retargeting

This is where the real magic happens. By installing the Facebook Pixel on your website, you can show ads specifically to people who have already visited your site. They already know you exist - a reminder ad with a strong offer can be what pushes them to book or buy.

You do not need an agency charging $800 to $2,000 per month to run effective Facebook ads. With the right setup, you can manage it yourself.

Respond to Every Message and Comment

This one sounds obvious, but it is often the difference between getting more customers from Facebook as a small business and just having a page that sits there doing nothing.

When someone comments on your post or sends you a message, they are raising their hand. They are interested. How quickly and how warmly you respond often determines whether they become a customer or go elsewhere.

A few practical tips:

- Set up a simple auto-reply for Facebook messages so people get an instant response, even outside business hours - something like "Thanks for reaching out! We will get back to you within a few hours."

- Reply to every comment, even if it is just a thank-you

- When someone leaves a positive review, respond personally and specifically

- Address negative feedback calmly and professionally - how you handle a complaint publicly says a lot about your business

Facebook also shows your average response time on your page. A "Responds within an hour" badge builds trust with potential customers before they have even spoken to you.

Track What Is Working and Adjust

You do not need to become a data analyst, but checking your Facebook Insights once a month is worth the ten minutes it takes. Look at which posts got the most reach and engagement, what times your audience is most active and whether your page followers are growing.

If a particular type of post consistently performs well - say, before-and-after photos for a cleaning business in Geelong, or recipe videos for a deli in Adelaide - do more of that. If something consistently gets ignored, stop wasting time on it.

The businesses that learn how to get more customers from Facebook as a small business are the ones that treat it like any other part of the business - they test, measure and improve over time.
