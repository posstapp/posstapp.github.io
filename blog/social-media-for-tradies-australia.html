Social Media for Tradies in Australia: Why It Works and How to Start

If you're a sparky in Parramatta, a plumber in Geelong, or a landscaper on the Gold Coast, chances are you've heard someone say "you need to be on social media." But between early starts, long days on the job, and everything else that comes with running your own trade business, posting on Instagram is probably the last thing on your mind.

Here's the thing though - social media for tradies in Australia is genuinely one of the best ways to find local work right now. And it doesn't have to eat up your time or your budget. This guide breaks down why it works, what platforms actually matter, and how to get started without overcomplicating it.

Why Social Media Works So Well for Tradies

Word of mouth has always been the lifeblood of trade businesses. Social media is basically word of mouth on steroids.

When a homeowner in Cronulla sees a before-and-after bathroom reno posted by a local tiler, they don't just scroll past. They save it, they tag their partner, and sometimes they send a message that same day asking for a quote. That's a lead you didn't have to chase.

A few reasons why this works so well for trades specifically:

- Your work is visual. A fresh coat of paint, a newly laid deck, a perfectly run cable setup - these things photograph beautifully and people respond to them.

- You're solving real problems. Homeowners are constantly looking for reliable tradies. When you show up online consistently, you become the obvious choice when they need someone.

- Local targeting is built in. You don't need a national audience. You need 50 people in your suburb to know your name, and social platforms make that very achievable.

Which Platforms Are Actually Worth Your Time

You don't need to be everywhere. Pick one or two and do them well.

Facebook

Still massive in Australia, especially for tradies. Facebook groups for local areas like "Northside Brisbane Buy Swap Sell" or "Inner West Sydney Locals" are goldmines. People post in these groups asking for tradie recommendations every single day. Having an active Facebook business page means when someone tags you in those threads, your page looks professional and trustworthy.

Facebook also has strong local ad targeting, which means if you want to run a simple $10 a day ad to reach homeowners within 15 kilometres of your base, you absolutely can.

Instagram

If your work is visual - and most trade work is - Instagram is worth your attention. Renovations, landscaping, tiling, painting, electrical fitouts - all of this performs well. You don't need a fancy camera. Your phone is fine. The key is consistency, not perfection.

Instagram Reels have been getting serious organic reach lately. A 30-second clip of you laying pavers or wiring a switchboard can easily reach a few thousand local people with zero ad spend.

TikTok

Growing fast, especially with younger homeowners. If you're comfortable on camera and want to explain what you do in a casual way, TikTok can work well. Not essential for every tradie, but worth knowing about.

What to Actually Post

This is where most tradies get stuck. They know they should post, but they sit there staring at their phone with no idea what to say.

Keep it simple. Here are content ideas that actually work for trade businesses:

- Before and after photos of jobs you've completed

- Short videos showing a problem you fixed and how you solved it

- Introductions to your team or your work vehicle

- Customer reviews (screenshot and share them)

- Behind-the-scenes content from the job site

- Tips homeowners can actually use (for example, a plumber in Melbourne might post "3 signs your hot water system is about to give up")

- Seasonal reminders ("Heading into summer - get your aircon checked before the heat hits")

You don't need to post every day. Three to four times a week is plenty to stay visible and build an audience over time.

Tips for Getting Started Without Overwhelm

If you're new to social media for tradies in Australia, here's a realistic approach that won't leave you burnt out after two weeks.

Start with one platform. If you're not sure which one, start with Facebook. It's where the most Australian homeowners still spend their time, and it's familiar.

Set up your business page properly. That means a clear profile photo (your logo or a photo of you on the job), a cover image, your contact details, your service area, and a short description of what you do. Many tradies skip this and wonder why nobody contacts them.

Take photos on every job. Get into the habit of snapping a photo when you arrive and when you're done. You don't need to post them immediately. Just build up a library so you always have content available.

Post consistently for 90 days before judging results. Social media is a slow burn. You're not going to post three times and get flooded with calls. But 90 days of consistent, useful content will absolutely start moving the needle.

Respond to every comment and message. Especially in the early days. One conversation in your inbox can turn into a booked job, a referral, or a repeat customer.

Common Mistakes Tradies Make on Social Media

Avoiding these will save you a lot of wasted effort.

Posting only promotional content. Nobody wants to follow a page that only ever says "call us for a free quote." Mix in helpful and behind-the-scenes content so people actually enjoy following you.

Inconsistent posting. Going quiet for three weeks and then posting five times in one day sends the wrong signal to both the algorithm and potential customers.

Ignoring comments and messages. If someone asks a question on your post and you don't reply for a week, that's a lead you just handed to your competitor.

No location information. If someone loves your work and wants to hire you, but can't figure out where you operate from, they'll move on. Make sure your service areas are clear everywhere.

Forgetting to ask for reviews. After a great job, ask your customer to leave a Google review and maybe share a photo you send them on their own Facebook. That kind of social proof spreads fast.
