Facebook vs Instagram for Small Business: Where Should You Post?

posst.app · July 2026 · 5 min read

If you're a small business owner trying to decide between Facebook and Instagram, here's the short answer: post to both. But if you're curious about which platform does what, and which one matters more for your specific type of business, read on.

Facebook in 2026: still the king for local business

Despite years of headlines predicting its decline, Facebook remains the most important social platform for Australian small businesses. The reason is simple: it's where local customers go to check if a business is real, active, and trustworthy.

Facebook's strengths for small business are rooted in its core features. Business Pages serve as a second website — complete with reviews, opening hours, contact details, and a feed of recent activity. When someone searches for "dog groomer Hawthorn" on Google, your Facebook page often appears alongside or instead of your website. If that page shows regular posts, positive reviews, and up-to-date information, you've just won a customer's trust before they even visit your site.

Facebook's demographic skews older than Instagram, which is actually an advantage for many local businesses. Adults aged 30-65 — the people booking services, hiring trades, and choosing restaurants — are highly active on Facebook. They use it to discover local businesses, read reviews, and get recommendations from friends and community groups.

Local community groups are another Facebook strength that Instagram simply doesn't match. Groups like "Northcote Community" or "Bayside Mums" are where word-of-mouth recommendations happen digitally. A business that posts regularly and is mentioned in these groups builds local authority faster than any ad spend.

Instagram in 2026: visual businesses and younger audiences

Instagram's strength is visual storytelling. If your business produces something beautiful, photogenic, or visually interesting, Instagram is where that content will perform best. Cafés, florists, hair salons, fitness studios, fashion retailers, interior designers, and photographers thrive on Instagram because their work naturally translates to compelling images.

The platform skews younger than Facebook — particularly strong with 18-40 year olds. If your target customer is in that range, Instagram is essential. It's also the platform where brand identity is built visually. Your grid — the collection of your recent posts — acts as a visual portfolio. People scroll through it quickly and form an impression of your business in seconds.

Instagram's discovery features are powerful for reach. Hashtags, the Explore page, and location tags all help new customers find your business. A post tagged with #maborncafe or #fitzroyflorist can surface your business to people who've never heard of you but are browsing content in your area.

The downside: Instagram is a more competitive, attention-hungry platform. Organic reach (how many people see your posts without paid promotion) has declined significantly. But consistent daily posting still works, especially for local businesses competing against other local businesses — not against global brands with massive budgets.

Which platform suits which business?

Facebook-first businesses: Trades (plumbers, electricians, builders), professional services (accountants, lawyers, financial planners), health practitioners (physios, dentists, GPs), automotive services, cleaning services, and real estate. These businesses serve an older demographic and rely heavily on reviews and local search visibility.

Instagram-first businesses: Cafés and restaurants, florists, hair and beauty salons, fitness studios, photographers, fashion retail, and interior design. These businesses have visually appealing products or environments that perform well in a feed.

Both equally: Pet services, child care and education, event venues, gyms, bakeries, and home renovation. These businesses have broad demographic appeal and visual content that works on both platforms.

Why "both" is the right answer

Here's the reality: whatever your business type, your potential customers are on both platforms. The plumber's clients might discover them on Facebook, but the plumber's future clients — younger homeowners buying their first property — are on Instagram. The café's Instagram-obsessed regulars also have Facebook accounts where they read community group recommendations.

Posting to both platforms doubles your visibility without doubling your work — especially when the content is automated. The same post, published simultaneously to Facebook and Instagram, reaches two different audiences with one piece of content. There's no strategic reason to limit yourself to one platform when the cost and effort of reaching both is the same.

The only scenario where you should genuinely pick one is if you're doing everything manually and truly can only manage one platform. In that case, go with Facebook for its local search benefits and broader demographic reach.

The consistency factor

Regardless of which platform you choose, the single most important factor is consistency. A Facebook page that posts daily will outperform one with a brilliant post every three weeks. An Instagram account with regular, decent content will build more followers than one with occasional stunning photos.

Both platforms' algorithms reward regular activity. They surface content from active accounts and suppress content from dormant ones. The business that posts every day — even if the content isn't perfect — will always win the visibility game against the business that posts occasionally with polished content.

The best platform is the one you actually post on. Regularly.
