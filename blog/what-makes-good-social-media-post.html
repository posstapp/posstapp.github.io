What Makes a Good Social Media Post? A Guide for Local Businesses

posst.app · July 2026 · 5 min read

Forget everything you've read about viral content strategies, growth hacking, and engagement optimisation. If you're a local business — a café, a dog groomer, a plumber, a physiotherapist — the rules are different. You're not competing with influencers or global brands. You're competing with the other local businesses in your suburb for the attention of nearby customers.

And in that competition, a good post is simpler than you think.

Show up. Regularly.

The single most important quality of a good social media post for a local business is that it exists. That sounds obvious, but it's the part most businesses get wrong. They wait for the perfect photo, the perfect caption, the perfect moment — and end up posting nothing for weeks.

A decent post published today is infinitely more valuable than a perfect post that never gets made. Facebook and Instagram both reward consistent activity. Their algorithms surface content from accounts that post regularly and suppress accounts that go quiet. The business that posts every day will reach more people than the business that posts once a month, regardless of content quality.

For local businesses, the bar is lower than you think. Your followers aren't expecting Vogue-level photography or Hemingway-level copywriting. They want to know you're active, you're real, and you're open for business.

Be local

This is the single biggest advantage local businesses have over big brands and AI-generated generic content: you actually exist in a specific place. Use that. Mention your suburb. Reference local landmarks, events, weather, and seasons. Talk about the street you're on, the neighbouring businesses, the community you serve.

A post from a bakery in Northcote that says "Fresh sourdough coming out of the oven on High Street this morning — perfect weather for a warm loaf" is ten times more engaging to a Northcote resident than a generic "Fresh bread daily!" post. The local reference creates a connection. It tells the reader: this business is here, in my area, right now.

Local keywords also help with discovery. When someone searches Facebook for "bakery Northcote" or "dog groomer Brunswick," posts that include those location terms are more likely to surface. Every post is an opportunity to reinforce your local presence.

Have one clear point

The most common mistake in small business social media is trying to say too much in a single post. A post that promotes your services, announces a special, shares a tip, and asks a question all at once ends up communicating nothing effectively.

Good posts have one job. That job might be: share a useful tip related to your service, highlight a specific product or offering, show what a typical day at your business looks like, or simply remind people you exist and are open. Pick one, execute it clearly, and move on. Tomorrow's post can do the next thing.

Think of each post as a single sentence in an ongoing conversation with your local community. You don't need to say everything at once. You just need to keep showing up with something relevant.

Use images that look real

You don't need professional photography. In fact, overly polished images can feel inauthentic on social media, especially for small local businesses. What you need are images that feel genuine — a photo of your workspace, your team, your product, or your street.

If you're using AI-generated images, look for tools that produce natural, contextual visuals rather than generic stock-style images. The best AI images for local business social media are ones that could plausibly have been taken at or near your business — warm lighting, real-looking environments, and settings that match your industry.

The key rule: every post needs an image. Text-only posts get dramatically less reach on both Facebook and Instagram. Even a simple, imperfect photo will outperform a text-only update. If you can't take a photo today, use an AI-generated one. Just make sure there's always something visual.

Write like a human, not a marketer

The best-performing small business captions sound like a real person talking — because that's what your followers respond to. Avoid corporate jargon, marketing buzzwords, and overly formal language. You're not writing a press release. You're having a casual conversation with people in your community.

Compare these two captions for a dog grooming salon:

"We offer comprehensive grooming solutions for all breeds. Book your appointment today for a premium experience."

versus

"This golden retriever came in looking like a cloud and left looking like a movie star. Summer coat season is here — book in before we're booked out."

The second one works because it's specific, it's warm, and it sounds like something a real person would say. It tells a tiny story. It has personality. And it naturally includes a call to action without feeling pushy.

If you're not sure what tone to use, imagine you're texting a friendly customer. That's the voice.

Hashtags: useful but not magical

Hashtags help people discover your content, but they're not the growth hack they once were. On Instagram, 3 to 5 relevant hashtags is the sweet spot. On Facebook, 1 to 2 hashtags or none at all — Facebook's algorithm doesn't rely on hashtags the way Instagram does.

The most effective hashtags for local businesses are location-specific and service-specific. Things like #MelbourneCafe, #DogGroomingMelbourne, #NorthcotePlumber, or #FitzroyHairSalon. These connect you with people searching for exactly what you offer, in exactly the area you serve. Generic hashtags like #SmallBusiness or #InstaGood might get impressions from random accounts, but they rarely bring in local customers.

The content types that work for local businesses

If you're ever stuck on what to post, these content types consistently perform well for small businesses across Australia:

  • Tips and advice related to your service — a physiotherapist sharing a desk posture tip, a dog groomer explaining coat care, a plumber warning about common winter pipe issues.
  • Behind the scenes — your workspace, your process, your team. People love seeing how things get done.
  • Seasonal content — tie your posts to the time of year. Summer grooming tips, winter menu specials, spring cleaning services.
  • Local references — mention your suburb, nearby parks, local events, or community news.
  • Simple service reminders — "Did you know we do [specific service]?" Many of your followers don't know your full range of offerings.

Notice what's not on the list: viral trends, dance challenges, memes, or anything that requires you to be a content creator. You're a business owner. Your social media should reflect your business, not your entertainment skills.

The only rule that matters

Post regularly. That's it. A good social media post for a local business is one that goes live today, mentions your business and your area, has an image, and sounds like a real person wrote it. You don't need to be clever. You don't need to go viral. You just need to be there — consistently, reliably, every day.

The businesses that win on social media aren't the most creative. They're the most consistent.
